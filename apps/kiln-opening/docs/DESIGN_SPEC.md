# DESIGN_SPEC.md

## Product vision

Kiln Opening should feel like a **medium-weight interactive Euro**, not a simulation and not a heavy optimisation sandbox.

The memorable story of a session should be:

> “I planned a workshop around certain ceramics and techniques, read the other workshops' needs, committed pieces to the shared kiln, and then we all discovered what the firing produced.”

## Core pillars

### 1. Shared kiln is the centrepiece

The shared kiln is the game's primary interaction and identity.

Players should care about:

- which zones remain;
- what Glazes opponents loaded;
- what heat those opponents probably want;
- how much Wood other players may commit;
- whether to load early for position or wait for information.

Do not add digital conveniences that remove this inference.

### 2. Controlled uncertainty, not chaos

The V1.0.4 Fire deck contains -2/-1/0/+1/+2 in a 4/3/6/3/4 distribution. Zero is the most common result, the negative and positive sides are symmetric, and exact-heat firing is therefore favoured without making wider outcomes rare.

The larger strategic uncertainty still comes from other players' secret Wood contributions. Fire is resolved through the existing uncapped heat-difference system; planning, zone choice and abilities remain the tools for managing its wider range.

### 3. Market vs Imperial must both be viable

Market route:

- easier/flexible Orders;
- better Coin income;
- Coins fund Decoration and Craft Techniques;
- fewer workforce rewards.

Imperial route:

- harder Orders;
- less Coin income;
- Imperial Progress;
- one-time Coin stipends;
- larger Imperial Exhibition capacity and, at the top spaces, diversity prestige.

Imperial Progress should be attractive but not mandatory.

### 4. Kiln Tradition is starting identity; Techniques are chosen development

The five Kilns make players asymmetrical from setup.

Craft Techniques answer: “What does my workshop become this game?”

A player may own at most 2 Techniques so nobody becomes universally efficient.

### 5. Shifu = efficiency/flexibility, not every special power

The Shifu should be stronger than an Apprentice, but technical mastery should live in Kiln Traditions and Techniques.

Important example: Shifu no longer repositions loaded ceramics. Repositioning is the Firing Technique **Kiln Setting**.

### 6. Avoid mandatory transit resources

Refined Clay was removed because Clay → Refined Clay was a compulsory 1:1 worker-placement tax with little strategic choice.

Clay preparation is abstracted into Forming.

### 7. Keep the game readable

Prefer:

- 6 meaningful locations over many narrow ones;
- small permanent effects over combo-heavy engines;
- visible card requirements;
- few timing windows with explicit names.

Avoid:

- specialists;
- trained/untrained worker states;
- extra firing sub-systems such as airflow, vessel size, weather, kiln furniture until the core proves too shallow.

## Desired strategic identities

A winning workshop should plausibly be described as one of:

- Market-heavy, Technique-rich workshop;
- Imperial-focused larger workforce;
- high-quality kiln specialist;
- balanced hybrid.

The strongest test of balance is:

> A player who does not rush the first Imperial Apprentice must still have a credible winning path.

## Interaction philosophy

Preferred:

- worker-placement blocking;
- kiln-space competition;
- shared heat incentives;
- card-display competition;
- indirect market pressure.

Avoid direct attack, resource stealing, take-that, or kingmaking effects.

## Theme treatment

The game is inspired by Song ceramics but abstracts real production.

Historical authenticity is most important in:

- terminology;
- material/shape/glaze feel;
- court vs market prestige;
- the uncertainty and craft of firing.

It does not need to model every historical production stage.
